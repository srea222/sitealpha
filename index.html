How SiteAlpha scoring works
Every parcel gets a DC Probability Score (0–100) — the model's read on how likely this land is to be acquired for data center development. Eight weighted factors:
Power proximity & capacity 30
Regional demand momentum 20
Leading indicators (filings, LLC buys) 15
Fiber proximity 10
Water access 8
Parcel quality (size, shape) 7
Zoning friendliness 5
Price-to-potential gap 5
What the bands mean — and what to do:
80–100 Acquire/Option — top conviction. Diligence immediately; option before the market re-rates.
60–79 Watch — strong candidate. Verify flood, rail, mineral rights; open owner conversations.
40–59 Monitor — not yet. Re-scores monthly as power & filings change.
<40 Pass — a hard exclusion or weak fundamentals.
FAQ
Why is nothing above 79?
Fiber and water factors are conservatively stubbed at neutral until those datasets are wired in. Scores can only go up from here — that's deliberate.

What makes a 76 valuable?
A 76 in the Watch band means: excellent grid access, inside an active corridor, big contiguous acreage — at ranch-land prices. The gap between that price and data-center-adjacent comps is the trade.

Has the model ever called one right?
Backtested: scoring NE Louisiana with only pre-announcement data, the model's #1 cell is where Meta later built its $27B Hyperion campus. The glowing zones on the Louisiana map are that run's output.

Where does the data come from?
County parcel records (via Regrid), HIFLD grid infrastructure, FEMA flood maps, utility & PSC filings. Demo parcels are labeled illustrative.
